# Come Look

## Spread 1

Ruth reads to the children at a small church on Sunday mornings. She has been doing it for twenty years.

## Spread 2

She knows what makes a lesson land. Which sentence to slow down on. Which picture to hold up one beat longer than feels comfortable. When to stop talking and let a seven-year-old finish the thought herself.

## Spread 3

When it works she leans over the page and says two words. Come look.

## Spread 4

Nobody has ever asked her to write any of it down. In twenty years it has never once needed to leave her head.

## Spread 5

Then the church asks for a book. Their own lesson, made into something the children can carry home in a bag.

## Spread 6

She gets a helper for it. The helper is quick, and it is willing, and it asks her everything.

## Spread 7

Which picture. How long. Is this one right. Come look, it says. Come look. Come look. Forty times before lunch.

## Spread 8

By the evening there is a book, and Ruth is tired in a way that has nothing to do with her arms. She did not make it. She answered for it, all day, one question at a time.

## Spread 9

So she writes it down. A page of it, by the door. Keep the sentences short. Never explain the ending. One idea to a page.

## Spread 10

The helper reads the page. It agrees with the page. Then it explains the ending.

## Spread 11

She writes it bigger. She underlines it. Same page, same morning, and the ending explained again.

## Spread 12

A written rule asks you to remember it. Remembering is the part that fails.

## Spread 13

So Ruth stops writing rules and starts building them.

## Spread 14

Not a note that says keep the sentences short. A frame a long sentence will not fit inside.

## Spread 15

Not a reminder about one idea to a page. A tray with room for exactly one card, so the second one has nowhere to go.

## Spread 16

Every one of them came out of a book that went wrong. She built one after each wrong book, for a long time. It was not interesting. It was the job.

## Spread 17

And at the end of the bench she put someone small, who reads the standard out of a book anyone is allowed to open.

## Spread 18

It has no opinion about her. It stops her own work, and it does. It lets far more through than it stops, because a gate that only ever refuses is a wall.

## Spread 19

One Sunday night Ruth said what she wanted and went to bed.

## Spread 20

Nothing woke her. There was nothing left to ask. Every question worth asking had already been built into the room.

## Spread 21

In the morning there was a book on the bench. And two words. Come look.

## Spread 22

She came. She read it the way she reads everything, slowly, with a finger under the line. Nobody has built a wall for that part, and she is not certain anyone should.

## Spread 23

The children know none of it. They know there is a book on Sunday, and it is theirs, and it is good.

## Spread 24

Come look.
