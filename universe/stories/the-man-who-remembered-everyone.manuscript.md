# The Man Who Remembered Everyone

## Spread 1

My friend called on a Tuesday to tell me his mother had died.

## Spread 2

I said all the right things. I never said her name, because I did not know it. He had told me twice.

## Spread 3

That is what a conversation was for me. It happened, and it was real, and then it was gone.

## Spread 4

I was not careless about it. I had voice memos on my phone, notes in one app, a recorded call in another. None of them talked to each other, and I never opened any of them again.

## Spread 5

So I stopped trying to remember. I kept it instead.

## Spread 6

Every recording became a plain file with a plain name, in one folder, on my own machine. Nothing clever. Just kept.

## Spread 7

The folder was not the point. Being able to read every file in it at once was the point.

## Spread 8

So I gave it to something that reads. It does not remember anything, and it never needs to. It reads the folder every time.

## Spread 9

What came back was a wall of small lights, one for each person I have, holding everything they ever told me.

## Spread 10

Most of them were dark at first. I had twenty years of people and almost none of it kept.

## Spread 11

It fills faster than you would think. A month of ordinary calls is a lot of light.

## Spread 12

Eleven months later, the same friend called.

## Spread 13

Before I picked up, I knew what he was carrying, and I knew her name was Ruth.

## Spread 14

I said it first. He went quiet, and then he cried, and we talked for an hour about her.

## Spread 15

People tell me this saves me hours every week. It does. That is the smallest thing it does.

## Spread 16

I am not better at remembering than I was. I keep it, and I can find it, and so nobody I love has to be a stranger to me twice.
