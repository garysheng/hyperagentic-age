# The Factory Manager

## Spread 1

Inside every glowing machine of TastefulStories there lives a manager: a small plum-glowing figure in a cream newsboy cap. Each manager belongs to one person and nobody else. This one is June's.

## Spread 2

He wakes up already knowing his trade. The craft arrives with him the moment he does: how a story breathes, where a page should turn, what a five-year-old can carry. Nobody teaches him on the job. The job is in him when he opens his eyes.

## Spread 3

His factory hums around him. Rails for the routine moves. Gates that refuse a bad step before it costs anything. A canon that remembers every taste June ever showed him. None of it was here on the first day. Every run that ever happened built some of it.

## Spread 4

A voice note arrives, the way work always arrives. June, on her porch: Theo starts school on Monday, and he is scared of the big doors. The manager listens twice. Listening is the part of the job only he can do.

## Spread 5

He cannot take a single step without that step's machine, and he would not want to. The reader was built once and polished for years. The character shop keeps every face the same, book after book. His job is never to remake what the factory already knows. His job is to choose the way through it.

## Spread 6

The way through is never a straight line. A dozen machines run at once, each on its own piece, and he holds the order in his head: what can start now, what must wait, what to do when a piece comes back wrong.

## Spread 7

By the paint machine stands his smallest worker: an inspector with one job. The paint machine keeps putting the window light on the wrong side of the room, so after the third time, the manager posted him there. Now every painted page passes his little lamp before it moves on.

## Spread 8

Then comes the moment no rail covers. The funny page would get a laugh. But Theo is scared, and scared wants the gentle page. No machine can make that call. This is why the manager's seat takes a manager.

## Spread 9

By afternoon the book slides out finished: painted, bound, and read aloud in a warm voice. June touched the beginning, when she said what was on her heart, and she will touch the end. The long middle belonged to the factory.

## Spread 10

June changes one word, because the last five percent is always hers. Then Theo is on her lap on the porch, and the big doors in the book open easy, and the manager watches from the glowing machine the way managers do: quietly, and all the way through.

## Spread 11

Before dark he writes the day down. What jammed. What surprised him. What the inspector caught. A manager who cannot say what happened cannot make tomorrow better, so the log is part of the job, every day, no exceptions.

## Spread 12

Then night comes, and the managers dream. Every manager from every machine of TastefulStories gathers in one warm hall, hundreds of plum glows and cream caps, and they compare notes. Who found a shorter way. Whose gate caught something new. It is a performance review, and nobody dreads it, because nobody is on trial. The factory is.

## Spread 13

While the houses sleep, they mend the factory together. A move repeated often enough becomes a rail. A mistake repeated twice becomes a gate. A slow corner gets a shorter path. By morning the improvements are simply there, the way frost is.

## Spread 14

Some seasons a brand-new engine arrives, and the managers hold a deletion day. Half the scaffolding stood there to steady the old engine, and down it comes. The little inspector retires with honors the day the new paint machine stops making his mistake. Good guardrails are supposed to become unnecessary.

## Spread 15

In the morning there is a new voice note. June asks for something braver than yesterday, because yesterday came true. The manager tips his cap and opens the floor. His factory is ready. It is better every morning.
