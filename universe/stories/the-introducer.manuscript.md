# The Introducer

## Spread 1

Every room has someone standing near the snack table who knows nobody. David finds them first. He crosses the room with his hand already out and says the four words he was born to say. Let me introduce you.

## Spread 2

Running communities taught David what most builders never learn. Nothing is obvious to a newcomer, and if you make people feel stupid you lose them. So he watches the edges of every room and asks one question. Who is falling through the cracks?

## Spread 3

He believed a room could be built to catch people, so he taught software his own instinct. His company learned to notice which two strangers ought to meet, then made the introduction for him, a thousand times a night. It was called Intros. Of course it was.

## Spread 4

The company worked, and a bigger one bought it. The sale proved a thing David had known for years. Bringing people together is real work, as real as steel or code, and it is worth real money.

## Spread 5

Then the tools changed, and a new kind of person appeared. A hyperagent is a person whose judgment runs through a team of tireless AI agents. The person supplies the taste, the values, and the call. The agents supply the hours.

## Spread 6

Here is the secret about all those agents. Idle, they do nothing. Every one of them is waiting on a human who knows what should exist and why it matters. The person is the source. The agents are the speed.

## Spread 7

So when an AI lab came calling, David did the math out loud. Of the companies that could do something about the risks of AI, Anthropic sits front and center. He chose the place where his effort would count the most, and he got to work.

## Spread 8

A few years ago one person could look after a handful of startups. On Anthropic's Applied AI team, David looks after about ninety at once. Ask him how and he grins. Agents, he says. I could not have done this before.

## Spread 9

His old craft came with him. Founders arrive nervous, certain that everyone else already understands this stuff. David makes it feel like brunch. Nothing is obvious, nobody is stupid, the door is propped open. Founders, meet your agents. Let me introduce you.

## Spread 10

Watch what happens when a founder wakes up to it. In the winter she is buried in work only she can do. By spring her agents carry that load, and she spends her days on the part that needs her heart. Multiply her by ninety companies and you can feel the ground move.

## Spread 11

Underneath the career sits the thing David actually believes. One of the finest human qualities is that we do not have to wait for our evolution. We can look at what we are, decide what we would rather become, and build the bridge on purpose. Intention, meet evolution. Let me introduce you.

## Spread 12

For the record, the introducer's own best chapter began the ordinary way, with two people meeting. David married Mikaela, and anyone who has watched them laugh together knows the theory holds. Good rooms, good companies, good lives. Each one starts with the right introduction.

## Spread 13

What works in a room works in the world. A society is a community of communities, and it has edges too, crowded with people slipping through cracks. Hyperagents can build the systems that notice them, welcome them in, and waste nobody. That is the world worth building toward.

## Spread 14

The coming years belong to people like this. Teachers with a thousand patient helpers. Doctors whose agents read every new paper overnight. Neighbors who can organize a block as easily as a group chat. People who choose their evolution on purpose, and choose it for the rest of us too.

## Spread 15

So this book is an introduction too, the kind David never thinks to make for himself. Reader, here is a man who spends his days connecting people to people, founders to the future, and humanity to its own next chapter. David, meet everyone. Everyone, meet David. Let me introduce you.
