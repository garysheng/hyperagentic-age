# The Best News Ever

*A HYPERAGENTIC AGE story*

**Spine:** thesis. The argued object is what winning means.
**Refrain:** Copy it.
**The promise, baked as readable lettering wherever it appears (exact string):**
`NEVER HAND OUT MORE FIRE THAN YOU CAN MAKE SAFE`

---

**1.**
There was a fire that could do anything, and everyone who had it was running.

**2.**
They were running at each other. Whoever got there first would own the thing that owned the world, so nobody slowed down, because slowing down was how you lost.

**3.**
A builder named Dario slowed down.

**4.**
He wrote a promise on the door of his workshop, where anyone walking past could read it. Never hand out more fire than you can make safe.

**5.**
It was not a slogan. It named the exact moment he would stop, and what he would do when he got there, and he wrote that part down too, so that later he could not quietly decide he had meant something softer.

**6.**
The other runners read his door and understood immediately what it was. It was a weight. He had picked up a weight and tied it to himself in front of everybody.

**7.**
So they laughed, and they ran, and for a while the laughing was fair, because he was slower.

**8.**
Then he did the thing nobody expected, which was not that he kept his promise. It was what he did next.

**9.**
He published the plans. Every measurement, every test, the whole method for making the fire safe, laid out so plainly that a competitor could take it without asking and use it by morning.

**10.**
His own people asked if he understood what he had given away. He said he understood exactly. Then he said the two words he would keep saying for the rest of his life. Copy it.

**11.**
Because a rule that only one workshop follows is not a rule. It is a handicap. The only way a promise becomes the way things are done is if the people you are racing take it from you.

**12.**
For a long time, nobody took it.

**13.**
They took his customers instead. They took his best people, some of them, with offers he could not match while carrying a weight.

**14.**
And one winter the moment his promise had named actually arrived, and the promise did what he had written it to do. He stopped.

**15.**
He stopped in public, with the work finished and waiting, while everyone who had not made a promise kept going. That was the most expensive day of his life and he had scheduled it himself, years earlier, in writing, before he knew what it would cost.

**16.**
The first workshop to copy him did not do it because they admired him. They did it because a customer asked why their door was blank.

**17.**
That was all. One question, from someone with money, about a door.

**18.**
They copied it badly at first, and half of it was for show. He was glad anyway. When his people said it was not sincere, he said sincerity was not the point, and that a rule you follow for your own reasons still holds when you would rather it did not.

**19.**
Then another workshop copied it, and then it was cheaper to have the promise than to explain why you did not, and then the people who wrote the laws simply wrote down what the good workshops were already doing.

**20.**
And the weight he had tied to himself stopped being a weight, because everyone was carrying one, and a race where everyone carries the same weight is only a race again.

**21.**
He lost his advantage completely. It had taken years and it had cost him more than he ever said out loud, and it was the plan.

**22.**
Somebody asked him, near the end of all that, whether it bothered him that the thing he built was now everybody's and his name was not on it.

**23.**
He said it was the best news he had ever gotten.

**24.**
Years later a young builder learns the safe way on her first morning. Her teacher explains it once, the way you explain which side of the road to drive on, and she nods and goes to work and never thinks about it again. On the door behind her, where she has stopped noticing it, someone has painted the promise. It is on every door on the street. She could not tell you who wrote it first, and that is the whole of what he wanted.
