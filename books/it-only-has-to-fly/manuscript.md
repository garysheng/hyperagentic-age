# It Only Has to Fly

*A HYPERAGENTIC AGE story. 17 spreads, landscape full-spread.*

*Refrain: **It does not check what it looks like. It checks whether it flies.***

---

**1.** Mira folded a paper plane on a Tuesday.

She went up on the step and threw it. It crossed the whole yard and landed by
the gate.

---

**2.** Her brother wanted one too.

So she folded another. Same paper. Same hands. Ten minutes later.

It went up, turned over, and dived into a bush.

---

**3.** By Friday there were five.

One flew beautifully. One went straight down. One only worked if you threw it
sideways, which is not really working.

Mira could not tell you why. She could only tell you after.

---

**4.** She sat on the step for a long time.

She wanted every plane to fly.

She did not want every plane to be the same plane. That would be a very boring
sky.

---

**5.** She had a helper. She called it Pip, which is not its real name, but it
answered to it.

She said: help me build a thing that makes planes.

I do not know how. I only know two things. **Every one has to fly. And I do not
want them all to be the same.**

---

**6.** Pip said: then I should not be checking what they look like.

Mira had not thought of that. She had been about to draw the good plane and say
copy this.

---

**7.** So instead they wrote down why a plane flies.

The nose has to be heavier than the tail.

Both wings have to match each other.

The creases have to be sharp.

Three sentences, and not one of them says what a plane looks like.

---

**8.** Then a strange problem.

To find out whether a plane flew, somebody had to throw it. And Mira threw
harder on good days.

So the first machine was not for planes at all. It was an arm. One arm, one
spring, the same throw every time.

You cannot test a thing with a ruler that keeps changing.

---

**9.** Now the good part.

Her brother folded something that was not a plane at all. It was fat and short
and had a folded-over nose, and Mira would have thrown it away.

The arm threw it. It flew further than anything she had made all week.

---

**10.** So it went in the sky, and it went into the book of planes that work.

Nobody had ever made that one before. Nobody had said it was allowed.

It flew, so it was allowed.

---

**11.** Some things still had to be shown, not said.

Mira tried to write down how to make a crease sharp. Three paragraphs. Pip read
all three and made something soft that opened in the air.

So she folded one and left it on the nail. Then she folded a second and opened
it out flat, all the creases showing, like a map.

After that Pip understood sharp.

---

**12.** What they built, in the end, was a line.

Cut the paper. Fold, however you like. Sharpen. Throw once. Fly.

And the line will not skip a step. A plane that never met the arm never gets to
the sky.

---

**13.** The line does not know what a plane is supposed to look like.

That was on purpose, and it took Mira a while to be brave about it.

It only knows what makes one fly.

---

**14.** One rainy week the paper went soft and no crease would hold.

Nobody had thought about rain. So they added a small machine that touches the
paper first and stops if it is damp.

Every machine in that shed is something that went wrong once.

---

**15.** Her brother came and looked at the whole line and said:

If a machine decides, is it still ours?

---

**16.** Mira said: Pip does not decide.

Pip wanted to help from the first day. It was not good at it yet, and that was
never Pip's fault. Nobody had told it what good meant.

So we told it. And then Pip was better at this than any of us.

But Pip has never once decided what a plane is **for**. What it is made of, what
it looks like, who it is for, whether anyone has ever seen one before.

**All of that is still us.**

---

**17.** That afternoon a hundred planes went up off the roof together.

No two of them the same. Fat ones, thin ones, one with a folded-over nose, one
somebody made out of a bus timetable.

Every single one of them flew.

**It does not check what it looks like. It checks whether it flies.**

---

## Closing plate

*A rule about what a thing must look like can only give you what somebody already imagined.*

*A rule about why it works can let in something nobody has thought of yet.*
