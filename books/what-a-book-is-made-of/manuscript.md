# What a Book Is Made Of

*A HYPERAGENTIC AGE story. 25 spreads, landscape full-spread.*

*Marcus, in his own words. A marketer with no theory of children's books ends up
with four hundred files describing what one is.*

*Refrain: **I could tell. I could not say.***

---

## How this book is drawn

**The factory is not a building. It is all happening inside his computer**, so it
is drawn the universe's own way: Marcus sits in a warm ordinary room at a desk,
and the work lives INSIDE the glowing amber laptop in front of him, where the
Chief of Agents and the sub-agents run the line. Late in the book the factory is
packaged as STORYWELL, a managed agent of its own, and a machine running Storywell
glows DEEP INDIGO rather than the Chief's orange, because in this universe the
colour of the glow tells you whose craft is running on it. He never goes in. His half of
every picture is the real room: the taste, the decision, the objection. Their
half is the screen.

That composition is the argument. He is outside it the whole way through, and it
is still his.

---

**1.** For a year I bought children's books about God for my two kids.

Some were beautiful. Most were instructive. None of them were bad.

Something was missing and I could not have told you what.

**I could tell. I could not say.**

---

**2.** One Saturday in November I thought: I could probably make one.

So I described Jonah the way I tell it at bedtime, and asked for a book.

---

**3.** It made one that afternoon.

Pictures, nineteen pages, a page you turn with your thumb.

Both my kids asked for it again on Monday, which is the only review that matters
in this house.

---

**4.** The next weekend they wanted the burning bush.

Second book. Just as good. And the two of them had nothing to do with each
other. The words sat somewhere else on the page. Moses and Jonah did not look
like they came from the same world.

I had always assumed the trouble with these machines would be that the work was
bad.

The work was good. Two good things do not make a pair.

---

**5.** By the fifth book I had five of them, and no two matched.

Every one had been worked out from nothing by something that did not know the
other four existed.

I was paying, every time, to decide things I had already decided.

---

**6.** So I said the question out loud, sitting on the stairs.

**If I make them all the same way, do I lose the thing that made them good?**

I sat with that for days, because I did not want the answer to be yes.

---

**7.** Then I stopped asking for a book.

I said what I was actually doing. I want to build a factory that makes these.
I do not know what that means yet and I am probably wrong about most of it.

**Two things have to stay true. They have to be as good as the good ones. And
they all have to open on one shelf.**

Work out the rest with me.

---

**8.** After that I stopped getting answers and started getting questions.

It could not leave a thing undecided.

So every place it had been quietly guessing turned out to be a place I had an
opinion and had never once said it.

---

**9.** First I wrote down what was not allowed to move. Three lines.

It held for about a week.

Then a book came out wrong anyway, because a line on a list has no hands.
Somebody still had to notice, and the somebody was me.

---

**10.** Naomi kept coming out wrong.

So I wrote a description of her face. Then a better one. Then one longer than
the story.

She still turned up as a different woman on page eleven.

---

**11.** So we stopped describing her and started showing her.

One picture, locked, and every page checked against it.

That worked, and it worked because it was not a rule. It was a thing that runs.

And a thing that runs can refuse.

---

**12.** Then she turned her head, and drifted again.

One picture of a face tells you nothing about the side of it.

So it stopped being a picture and became a sheet. Front, three-quarter, profile,
a few faces she pulls. Enough that a new angle is something she has already
been.

And the checking had to lean right in, close, one thing at a time. From far
enough away, a wrong face looks fine.

---

**13.** Once something could refuse, order turned up out of nowhere.

You cannot check a face against a locked picture if nothing has been locked yet.

So the locking has to happen first. So something has to decide who is even in
this book before that.

---

**14.** That is where I stopped writing rules and started building a line.

Stations, and a book that walks down them. Each one will not start until the
thing it needs already exists.

A wrong face stopped being something I catch at the end.

**It became something the factory cannot make.**

---

**15.** The line looks complicated now. That is not clever design.

Every station on it is something that went wrong once, standing exactly where it
went wrong.

Four hundred files, and I never sat down and wrote four hundred of anything. I
answered one question, then another, most evenings, for a few months.

---

**16.** One evening it told me two of my own rules could not both be true.

One of them meant nine pages. The other meant twenty-six. Both perfectly
reasonable on their own.

Writing the parts down was never the job. Writing parts that do not fight each
other is the job, and I could not see the fight from inside the thing I was
building.

---

**17.** Then I saw something I had walked straight past seventeen times.

A book is pages. Open a page and there is a picture. Open the picture and there
is a girl in it.

She is not part of that page. She walks through fourteen of them.

She was a thing all along, at her own level, and I had never written down that
she was a thing.

---

**18.** A church asked for one at Easter, in their own words. Then two more.

So I asked the real question.

**How do I package all of this up and send it out to a church without me
standing in the middle of it?**

---

**19.** That turned out to be the product.

Not a form. Not a website with boxes to fill in.

**A church gets its own Storywell.**

---

**20.** The first thing I got wrong about it was where it lives.

It is not on my laptop. I close the lid and go to bed, and it carries on without
me.

A thing that only runs while your machine is awake is not a thing you can give
somebody.

---

**21.** And it does not get a summary of the factory. It gets the factory.

The same line, the same order, the same small workers, running now on a machine
in a church office I have never been inside.

---

**22.** It asks the way a person asks.

One church talked to it for forty minutes and sent four paragraphs about a boy
in their congregation who had had a hard year.

---

**23.** Another typed six words and left.

Both of them got a whole book.

---

**24.** That is the opposite of what people expect from being careful.

All that answering did not turn into a longer form. It turned into a list of
every decision a finished book needs.

Once that list exists, each one can come from the church, or it can be made
without them. **A vague tool has to ask you everything, because it cannot tell
what is missing. A precise one can offer to decide.**

---

**25.** I was a marketer. I had two kids, a shelf that never quite landed, and
nothing but the ability to tell.

**I can say it now.**

---

## Closing plate

*You do not need the theory before you start.*

*You need something that keeps asking, and will not take a mood for an answer.*
